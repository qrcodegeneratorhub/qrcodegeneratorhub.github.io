QR Codes for Real Estate: Listings, Signs and Open Houses

A code on a for-sale sign turns a drive-by into a viewing. How agents use QR codes to sell homes faster.

A person driving past a house for sale has about ten seconds to decide whether it is worth a closer look. A QR code on the sign gives them the answer in one scan: photos, a virtual tour, the price, and the agent's number, without them having to stop the car or remember an address. Real estate is one of the best fits for QR codes, because the customer is standing in front of the product, phone in hand, with a clear question. This guide covers the placements that work, the content that converts, and the mistakes that waste a good sign.

The for-sale sign: your best placement

A QR code on a for-sale sign should link to the property's own page, with photos, a floor plan, the price, and a way to contact the agent or book a viewing. Make the code large — at least 15 centimetres wide, because it will be scanned from a car or from across the street — and print it on a rigid board that does not flutter in the wind. Use a high error correction level, because an outdoor sign gets dirty and weathered. Add a short instruction, such as Scan for photos and tour, so passers-by know what they will get. And use a different code for each property, so you can tell which signs are generating interest.

Open houses: from visitor to lead

At an open house, a QR code at the door can open a sign-in form, so visitors register without a clipboard and a queue. A code in each room can link to details about that room — the renovation, the appliances, the view — which is more engaging than a printed sheet. A code on the information sheet can link to the virtual tour, so visitors can revisit the house from home and share it with a partner who could not attend. Keep every form short: name, email, and one question about what they are looking for. A long form at the door will be abandoned, and an abandoned form is a lost lead.

Brochures, flyers and direct mail

A QR code on a brochure or a flyer lets the reader jump to the property page, the virtual tour, or the mortgage calculator, which is far more useful than a printed address they must type. For a development of several homes, a code on the brochure can link to a page where the reader chooses the plot they are interested in. For direct mail, a code linking to a valuation tool — How much is your home worth? — generates leads from homeowners who are not yet selling but are curious. In each case, the code should lead to the next step, not to the agency's homepage, and the page it opens must work well on a phone.

Virtual tours and video

A QR code is the natural bridge between a physical sign and a virtual tour. A short video walkthrough, viewed on a phone, lets a buyer who is standing outside the house see the inside immediately, which is often the difference between driving on and calling the agent. Keep the video short — two or three minutes — and make sure it loads quickly on mobile data. A code that opens a slow, heavy virtual tour will be abandoned. Put the tour code on the sign, on the brochure, and on the listing itself, so the buyer can find it wherever they encounter the property.

Measuring what works

Give each property and each placement its own destination address, so you can see which signs, brochures and open houses are generating views and enquiries. Track not just scans but viewings booked and offers made, because a code that is scanned often but produces no viewings is pointing at the wrong content. Review the numbers monthly and move your effort toward the placements that convert. And remember to remove or replace codes when a property sells: a code on a sold sign that leads to a dead page wastes the next buyer's time and makes the agency look careless.

Frequently asked questions

How big should a QR code on a for-sale sign be?

At least 15 centimetres wide, so it can be scanned from a car or across the street. Use a high error correction level for outdoor signs.

What should a property QR code link to?

The property's own page with photos, a virtual tour, the price and a way to book a viewing — not the agency homepage.

Can QR codes capture leads at an open house?

Yes. A code at the door linking to a short sign-in form collects visitor details without a clipboard or a queue.
