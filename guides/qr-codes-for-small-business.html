QR Codes for Small Business: 12 Practical Uses

From menus to payments to reviews, QR codes are the cheapest marketing tool a small business can buy. Here are twelve that actually work.

Small businesses rarely have a marketing budget, which is exactly why QR codes are so useful: they cost nothing to create, nothing to print beyond the paper, and they turn every surface you already own — a counter, a receipt, a window, a delivery box — into a working link to your business. The trick is not to use QR codes everywhere, but to use them where they remove friction: where a customer is standing, phone in hand, and one scan saves them typing, searching or asking. These twelve uses are the ones that pay for themselves fastest.

1 to 4: menus, ordering and payments

A restaurant can put a QR code on each table linking to the menu, so the menu is always current and never needs reprinting when prices change. The same code can link to an ordering page, letting customers order and pay from their seats. A shop can put a payment QR code at the till for customers who prefer to pay by phone, and a service business can put a booking QR code on its door so passers-by can reserve a slot without calling. Each of these replaces a step that used to require staff time, paper, or a phone call, and each works while the business is closed as well as while it is open.

5 to 8: reviews, social media and loyalty

A QR code on a receipt or a table tent that links directly to your Google review page will generate far more reviews than a polite request, because it removes every step between the satisfied customer and the review form. A code on a business card that opens your Instagram or WhatsApp saves the customer from searching for your name and finding a similarly named competitor. A loyalty code that customers scan at each visit can replace a paper stamp card that gets lost in a drawer. And a code on packaging that links to a how-to video or a care guide turns a one-time purchase into an ongoing relationship with your brand.

9 to 12: information, feedback and hiring

A property agent can put a QR code on a for-sale sign linking to photos and a virtual tour. A tradesperson can put one on their van linking to a quote form. A shop can put one on a shelf edge linking to stock information or an extended range. And a business that is hiring can put a code in its window linking straight to the application form, catching people who are already standing outside the door. In each case the code answers the question the customer is asking at that exact moment, in that exact place — which is the whole point of a QR code.

Making codes that customers actually scan

A QR code only works if people notice it and trust it. Give it a clear instruction — Scan for the menu, Scan to pay, Scan to leave a review — because a bare code with no explanation is easy to ignore. Make it large enough to scan from where the customer stands, keep it high contrast, and test it on two different phones before printing a hundred copies. Put the code where the customer already has their phone out: at the till, on the table, on the receipt. And keep the destination mobile-friendly and fast, because a code that opens a slow desktop website will be abandoned before it loads.

Measuring whether it is working

Static QR codes have no built-in analytics, so measure them the simple way: use a different destination address for each placement, so you can see in your website statistics which code is being scanned. A menu code and a window code should point to different pages, or to the same page with a different tracking tag. Count the reviews, bookings or orders that arrive through the codes, and compare them with the cost — which is usually just the printing. If a code is not being scanned, the problem is usually placement or labelling, not the technology: move it, enlarge it, or explain it better. The codes that work are the ones that solve a problem the customer already has.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to use QR codes for my business?

Almost nothing. Generating codes on this site is free, and printing them costs only the paper and ink.

Do customers actually scan QR codes?

Yes, when the code solves a problem — a menu, a payment, a review. Codes without a clear purpose are ignored.

Can I track how many people scan my codes?

Static codes have no tracking, but you can point each code at a different web address and count visits to each one.
