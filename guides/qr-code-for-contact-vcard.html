Put Your Contact Details in a QR Code (vCard Guide)

One scan saves your name, number and email into a stranger's phone. How vCard QR codes work and how to make a good one.

Exchanging contact details used to mean a business card, a scribbled number, or a slow dictation of an email address. A vCard QR code replaces all of that: the other person points their phone at your code and your name, phone number, email and website appear as a contact they can save with one tap. It works at conferences, on business cards, on email signatures, and anywhere you meet people. This guide explains the vCard format, how to build a code that works on every phone, and the design choices that make people actually scan it.

What is a vCard?

A vCard is a standard text format for contact details, invented long before QR codes and supported by every phone and email program. It looks like a small block of text: BEGIN:VCARD, then lines for the name, organisation, phone, email and website, then END:VCARD. When those lines are encoded in a QR code, the scanning phone recognises the format and offers to save the contact. The format is deliberately simple and old, which is its strength: it works on iPhones, Android phones, and desktop scanners alike, without any app or account. The tool on this site builds the vCard for you; you just fill in the fields.

Which fields to include

Include the fields people actually need: your name, your phone number, your email address, and your company or job title. Add your website if you have one. Leave out anything sensitive or unnecessary — your home address, your personal email, or a second phone number you do not want shared widely. Remember that a QR code can be photographed by anyone who sees it, so treat it like a business card: share what you would print on a card, and nothing more. If your job changes, generate a new code; an old vCard code will keep giving out your old details until you replace it.

Making a code that scans everywhere

Keep the vCard short, because a long vCard makes a dense code that is harder to scan. Use the standard field names and avoid special characters that can confuse older scanners. Test the code on both an iPhone and an Android phone, and check that each field appears correctly — a phone number in the wrong field or an email address with a typo is worse than no code at all. Use error correction level M or higher, because a business card gets handled, bent and rained on. And print the code at least 2 centimetres wide on a card, with a clear margin around it and a short label such as Scan to save my contact.

Where to use a contact QR code

The classic place is the back of a business card, where it turns a card that might be lost into a contact that is saved in seconds. It also works on a conference badge, a name tag, a presentation's final slide, an email signature, and a LinkedIn profile header. For a shop or a practice, a code at reception lets visitors save the business's details without asking. For a freelancer, a code on a portfolio or a quote lets a client save the details at the moment they decide to call. In each case, the code removes the step where the other person has to type your details by hand — and typing is where errors happen.

Design tips for a professional result

A contact QR code is part of your professional image, so treat it like one. Print it in black on white for maximum reliability, and resist the urge to make it a colour that matches your brand but scans poorly. If you add a logo, keep it small and centred, and use a high error correction level so the code still reads. Put your name and job title next to the code, so people know whose details they are saving. And check the code every time you reprint your cards: a vCard code with an old phone number is worse than no code, because it sends people to a dead number with confidence.

Frequently asked questions

Will a vCard QR code work on any phone?

Yes. iPhone and Android both recognise the vCard format and offer to save the contact with one tap.

What details should I put in a contact QR code?

Your name, phone, email, company and website. Leave out anything you would not print on a business card.

Can I update a vCard QR code after printing it?

No, if it is a static code. Generate a new code with the new details and reprint the card.
