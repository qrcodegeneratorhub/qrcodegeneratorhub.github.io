How to Scan a QR Code on Any Phone

iPhone, Android, old phone, no app — every way to scan a QR code, and what to do when it will not scan.

QR codes are everywhere now — on menus, posters, tickets, packaging and receipts — and scanning one should be effortless. On most modern phones it is: open the camera, point it at the code, and tap the notification that appears. But not every phone behaves the same way, and older phones, different camera apps and unusual situations can all get in the way. This guide covers every common method, so you can scan a code on whatever phone you have, and know what to do when the obvious method fails.

iPhone: the built-in camera

On an iPhone, open the Camera app and point it at the QR code, holding the phone steady about 15 to 30 centimetres away. A yellow notification appears at the top of the screen showing where the code leads; tap it to open the link, join the Wi-Fi, or save the contact. If nothing appears, check that QR scanning is enabled in Settings, then Camera, then Scan QR Codes. You can also scan from the Control Centre by adding the QR code scanner button, or by long-pressing a QR code in a photo in your gallery. No extra app is needed, and the phone will warn you if the code points to a suspicious address.

Android: camera, Lens and quick settings

On most Android phones, the built-in Camera app scans QR codes the same way: point, wait for the prompt, tap it. If your camera does not offer this, open Google Lens — either from the camera app, from the Google app, or from the quick-settings tile — and point it at the code. Samsung phones also have a QR scanner in the quick-settings panel and in the Bixby Vision feature. Pixel phones scan codes directly in the camera app. If none of these work, download a reputable scanner app from the Play Store, such as the one from Kaspersky or the open-source Binary Eye, and avoid scanner apps that demand excessive permissions or show aggressive advertising.

Scanning a code on a screen or in a photo

Sometimes the QR code is on your own phone — in an email, a message, or a photo — and you cannot point your camera at your own screen. On an iPhone, save the image to your photos, open it, and long-press the code to get the option to open it. On Android, open the image in Google Photos and tap the Lens button, which will recognise the code. You can also take a screenshot of the code and scan the screenshot the same way. If the code is on another person's screen, hold your phone steady, turn up the other screen's brightness, and angle your phone to avoid reflections, which are the most common reason screen codes fail.

When a code will not scan

If a code will not scan, the problem is usually distance, light, or damage. Move closer or further away — most phones scan best at 15 to 30 centimetres for a small code — and make sure the code is well lit without glare. Clean the camera lens, which is often smudged. If the code is on a curved surface, such as a bottle or a cup, flatten the angle by moving to the side. If the code is very small, zoom in with the camera's pinch gesture, which helps on many phones. And if the code is damaged or faded, try a different angle: error correction lets a scanner reconstruct up to 30 percent of a damaged code, but only if the position markers in the three corners are intact.

Staying safe when you scan

A QR code is a link you cannot read before you open it, so a little caution is wise. Before tapping, read the notification: your phone shows the address the code leads to, and you should check that it matches what you expect. If a code on a parking meter or a restaurant table leads to a strange address, or asks you to download an app or enter payment details immediately, stop — the code may have been replaced with a malicious one. Never scan a code that promises free money, a prize, or an urgent account warning. And keep your phone's software updated, because updates patch the vulnerabilities that malicious codes could exploit.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to download an app to scan QR codes?

No, on almost all modern phones. The built-in camera app scans QR codes on iPhone and most Android phones.

Why won't my phone scan a QR code?

Usually distance, glare, a dirty lens, or a damaged code. Move closer, clean the lens, and try a different angle.

Is it safe to scan any QR code I see?

Almost always, but check the address your phone shows before tapping, and be wary of codes that demand payment or a download.
